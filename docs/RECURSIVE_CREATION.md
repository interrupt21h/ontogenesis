# Recursive creation: human intention, machine intelligence and computational emergence

*An artist’s statement on human intention, machine intelligence and computational emergence.*

## The process as medium

Ontogenesis is both an artificial world and an investigation into how such a world can be created. Its subject is emergence: the appearance of organized behavior through the interaction of comparatively simple local rules. Its medium, however, extends beyond numerical simulation. The work is constructed through an iterative exchange between human intention, generative artificial intelligence and the observable consequences of computational experiments.

I do not begin with a complete specification of the finished work. I begin with a question. I use AI to give that question computational form, then examine what the resulting system actually does. Its behavior often suggests questions I could not have formulated before the experiment. The mathematical system is subsequently extended, revised or instrumented. The work develops through this recurring cycle rather than through the execution of a fixed plan.

The individual animations are therefore manifestations of a larger artistic object: the evolving collection of models, measurements, images and decisions that constitutes Ontogenesis.

## Delegating complexity

A conventional computational artist must negotiate the distance between an imagined phenomenon and the technical machinery necessary to produce it. Mathematical formulation, numerical integration, implementation, debugging, visualization and publication all consume time and attention. Each imposes constraints on the breadth of an investigation.

I deliberately redistribute that labor in Ontogenesis. AI participates extensively in translating conceptual intentions into equations, software, instruments, visualizations and documentation. It also proposes mechanisms and possible directions for subsequent experiments. This is not simply automated typing or the execution of a fully specified design. A portion of the exploratory and constructive work itself is delegated.

I can consequently concentrate my attention on the questions that motivate the artwork: what properties should exist in the artificial world, what relationships are worth exploring, what constitutes an interesting outcome, and how those outcomes should be presented. The reduction in implementation friction makes it practical to pursue ideas that might otherwise remain speculative.

The complexity has not disappeared. It has been relocated into an AI-mediated production process, software infrastructure and numerical computation. I must still assess whether the resulting implementation corresponds to the intended inquiry. Delegation exchanges some direct control over implementation for a greater capacity to explore, and creates a corresponding need for verification.

## Three layers of evolution

Three distinct processes provide the conceptual structure of the work.

**Evolution within the artificial world.** Chemical concentrations interact and reorganize. Later models introduce inherited variation, selection, metabolic exchange and mechanisms through which populations modify their environment. These are computational analogues of selected biological processes, not a demonstration that the system is alive.

**Evolution of the artificial world.** Successive experimental generations inherit elements of prior mathematical models while introducing new mechanisms or instruments. The laws of the world change through deliberate revision. Each generation is a fresh seeded experiment rather than a literal continuation of the preceding population.

**Evolution of the creative inquiry.** I develop the investigation with AI through repeated cycles of proposal, implementation, observation and criticism. The outcomes of previous experiments influence the next questions, and the available instruments influence what can be observed and understood.

The three processes are related but not identical. Biological selection within a model is not equivalent to human selection among software revisions. Nor does the AI autonomously evolve the project without direction. For me, the artistic significance lies in the correspondence between these levels: a work concerned with emergence is itself produced through a contingent, cumulative process.

## A recursive creative method

The development cycle can be described in six stages.

1. **Intention:** I establish a question, constraint or desired conceptual direction.
2. **Synthesis:** AI proposes or implements a mathematical and computational realization.
3. **Execution:** the model produces numerical states, measurements and visual representations.
4. **Observation:** unexpected behavior, limitations and failures become visible.
5. **Evaluation:** I judge the scientific plausibility, artistic interest and relevance of the outcome, with AI-assisted interpretation where useful.
6. **Revision:** selected ideas and mechanisms become the basis for another experiment.

This is a recursive method in the ordinary creative sense: the output of one investigation informs the input of the next. It is not a claim that the software automatically rewrites itself through a formal recursive algorithm.

My process permits changes to the laws of the simulated world as well as to its instruments of observation. An apparent structure may motivate topology measurements; ambiguity in a moving image may motivate fixed-coordinate numerical sections. The observer is not external to the development of the work: the means of observation become part of its evolving architecture.

## Discovery, failure and the resistance of the model

My intention does not determine the outcome of an experiment. The interaction of numerical rules creates a form of resistance to intention. Adding a mechanism intended to support organization can instead produce saturation, collapse or the disappearance of structures at a selected threshold.

The progression from genetic variation to ecological coupling and structural permeability illustrates this principle. Structural saturation in Generation 0016 and the near disappearance of thresholded matrix in Generation 0017 motivate subsequent experiments on morphogenesis. Later factorial controls distinguish the effects of particular construction and maintenance configurations. These outcomes are not proof of general biological principles; their value is that they constrain interpretation and redirect the inquiry.

Failure is therefore material for the work, rather than simply a defect to conceal. The same distinction applies to the production process: an implementation failure is not an emergent scientific result, and a successful rendering is not proof of mathematical correctness.

## Authorship and distributed agency

I direct Ontogenesis. My artistic authorship rests in the sustained formulation of the inquiry, selection of directions, evaluation of results, decisions about presentation and responsibility for the published work. AI makes substantial contributions to the formulation and implementation of mechanisms, source code, analytical instruments and written material.

This division of labor should be visible rather than concealed. Describing every line of code as independently authored by me would misrepresent the process; describing the work as an autonomous AI creation would likewise erase the direction I provide. The project occupies a deliberately distributed mode of production.

AI is an intelligent computational intermediary in the practical sense of producing and transforming representations in response to instructions. This description does not require attributing subjective experience, independent artistic intention or consciousness to the model.

The approach has affinities with conceptual and generative art, in which instructions, systems and processes can be constitutive of the artwork. What interests me particularly is that the system of instructions is itself repeatedly reconsidered through AI-assisted inquiry. This is an artistic proposition, not a claim of historical priority over other practitioners.

## Engineering as artistic material

The source code, numerical archives, automated experiments and publication infrastructure are not merely ancillary technical products. They give the work continuity, traceability and a capacity for renewed examination. A new generation can preserve its predecessor while introducing a different question. Recorded numerical states can support representations that were not anticipated when the simulation was executed.

The distinction between artistic images and scientific measurements is essential. An animation can express the character of a computational world without exposing every quantitative property. A fixed section, concentration projection or topology measurement addresses a different question about the same world. Neither representation is exhaustive, especially when the domain has four spatial dimensions.

Reproducibility is thus an artistic as well as an engineering value: it allows the viewer to encounter the construction behind the image. It does not guarantee scientific validity. Numerical stability, model assumptions, controls, independent checks and the provenance of experimental data remain necessary.

## Acceleration and its limits

What particularly interests me about this process is how dramatically it compresses the interval between a conceptual question and an executable experiment. I can pursue a line of inquiry while it is still fresh, without having to resolve every implementation detail myself. It does not establish a measured productivity multiplier, nor does it eliminate the expertise involved in evaluating the result.

My technical literacy, mathematical interests and aesthetic judgment shape which proposals I accept, question or reject. AI can generate plausible but incorrect explanations, introduce implementation errors or obscure the difference between an observation and an inference. The speed of construction may exceed the speed of validation.

Consequently, the project distinguishes proposed mechanisms from executed experiments and verified measurements from interpretations. Historical models and recorded outcomes establish the actual trajectory of the work; planned capabilities should not be mistaken for completed results.

## An open-ended artwork

Ontogenesis has no necessary terminal form. A new mechanism can generate new behavior, new behavior can demand new instruments, and new instruments can transform the questions that guide subsequent generations. The work is not directed toward an inevitable increase in complexity or toward a predetermined definition of life.

My artistic proposition is that the capacity to construct computational worlds can become an extension of curiosity itself. By delegating substantial implementation complexity while retaining critical and aesthetic direction, I investigate not only what can emerge from mathematical rules, but also what can emerge from a recursive relationship between human imagination and machine intelligence.

The artificial world and the process by which it is constructed are inseparable parts of that inquiry.
