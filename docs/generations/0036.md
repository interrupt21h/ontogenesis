# Generation 0036 — Structural Feedback

*Status: Genuinely new numerical intervention, with a verified two-seed matched pilot at 12⁴ lattice sites and 120 integration steps. Not a demonstration of autonomous organisms.*

[← Experiment atlas](../EXPERIMENTS.md) · [Visual gallery](../VISUAL_GALLERY.md) · [Art direction](../ART_DIRECTION.md)

## The question

The earlier G0033–G0035 editions compared populations and visualized the structures already present in numerical concentration fields. G0036 changes the numerical algorithm itself: **what happens when the simulated structure and local species balance influence subsequent transport?**

It is a limited, well-defined experiment in *state-dependent conservative transport*. The existing integrator already accepts a `velocity_feedback` callback. G0036 computes that correction from the current native 4D matrix and V/W fields at every solver step. It does not modify stored footage, fake additional timesteps, or propose a new organism type.

## A true matched control

Both runs start with an identical pseudorandom initialization and use the same external complementary niche field (strength 0.70), V decay multiplier (0.97), numerical integration parameters, seed, and measurement schedule. The only difference is the enabled numerical feedback:

| Arm | Numerical transport intervention |
|---|---|
| `control` | The original 4D transport calculation, `velocity_feedback=None` |
| `feedback` | Original 4D transport plus a bounded curl-like correction derived from the current state |

The two independently initialized seeds are **42 and 73**. Both were sampled at **11 native numerical times**, steps 0, 12, 24, …, 120, on a **12×12×12×12** periodic grid. Their unmodified initial solver-state hashes are checked for equality, and later hashes must diverge for an intervention to be considered effective.

## Conservative state-dependent correction

The scalar feedback potential at lattice location \(x\) is

\[
\phi(x) = g\tanh\left(3[m(x)-0.12]+1.7[v(x)-w(x)]\right)
\]

where \(m\) is matrix density, \(v,w\) are the two numerical populations, and \(g=0.035\) is the prescribed feedback gain.

Let \(D_i\) denote the backward periodic difference along coordinate \(i\). The four components added to the original face-velocity field are

\[
\delta\boldsymbol{u} =
\left(D_1\phi,-D_0\phi,D_3\phi,-D_2\phi\right).
\]

These antisymmetric pairs are divergence-free because the periodic discrete differences commute:

\[
\sum_{i=0}^{3}D_i\delta u_i=0.
\]

This condition is independently tested against randomized four-dimensional states. It also holds inside the existing solver's velocity and flux constraints. **Divergence-free correction is a property of the transport term**, not a claim that the entire reaction system conserves mass, energy or biological individuals.

The correction is recomputed using the instantaneous solver state, making it actual feedback rather than an externally prerecorded trajectory.

## Native evidence and scope

The completed [matched numerical CI](https://github.com/interrupt21h/ontogenesis/actions/runs/45486825879) publishes a [two-seed aggregate summary](https://github.com/interrupt21h/ontogenesis/releases/download/atlas-media/g0036-summary.json) and each seed's original archive metadata. The summary provides measured V-active and W-active site counts, same-site activity, exclusive 4D periodic face contacts, matrix/energy means, and final mean absolute differences between matched full-field states.

The source archives contain the genuine recorded `v`, `w`, `m`, and `e` arrays for both treatment arms. SHA-256 identities tie the samples and renderers to the same numerical run.

The two-seed pilot is a **feasibility and sensitivity experiment**, not a replicated large-scale claim of emergent biological feedback. It establishes that the prescribed feedback intervention produces distinguishable numerical trajectories while preserving the correct discrete four-dimensional transport constraints.

## Independent eight-seed replication

The initial two-seed pilot was followed by the completed [G0036 eight-seed matched replication](https://github.com/interrupt21h/ontogenesis/actions/runs/45487798716). The separate replication reused the same original solver and state-derived feedback rule on seeds **42, 73, 101, 137, 211, 307, 401 and 509**, each at 12⁴ sites and 120 integration steps. Every run verified identical initial-state hashes between control and treatment, then retained read-only native measurements at steps 0, 60 and 120. The complete [numerical JSON](https://github.com/interrupt21h/ontogenesis/releases/download/atlas-media/g0036-replication.json) and [eight-seed measured effect figure](https://github.com/interrupt21h/ontogenesis/releases/download/atlas-media/g0036-replication.png) were published after all eight jobs and the aggregation checks succeeded.

The endpoint quantity below is the **mean absolute concentration difference across every native 4D lattice site** between feedback and control—not a population fitness score, 2D projected-image measurement, or beneficial-effect statistic.

| Native field | Median difference at step 120 | Minimum–maximum across eight seeds | Seeds with nonzero difference |
|:--|--:|:--|--:|
| V | 0.002853 | 0.000673–0.012161 | 8/8 |
| W | 0.001318 | 0.0000000344–0.012244 | 8/8 |
| Matrix | 0.001420 | 0.000758–0.005588 | 8/8 |
| Energy | 0.000512 | 0.000171–0.002314 | 8/8 |

This replication strengthens the conclusion that the prescribed structural feedback produces measurable numerical consequences across different initializations. The response is **heterogeneous**, including a very small W response in seed 42. Nothing in these absolute differences shows that the feedback was beneficial, self-selected, biologically stable or autonomously adaptive. The 120-step duration remains a finite pilot.
