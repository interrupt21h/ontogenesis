# Vision and method

Ontogenesis is an exploration of the relationship between rule, form and becoming. I approach computation as an artistic medium in which I specify conditions for development rather than drawing the resulting forms. I ask how far a small vocabulary of local interactions can be extended toward a world with differentiated regions, persistent structures, environmental memory and inherited variation.

## Purpose and temporal boundary

I began Ontogenesis as a brainstorming exercise: I wanted a complicated system that could be progressively evolved, generate visually interesting artifacts and fully exercise continuous integration (CI) as a creative production environment. Artificial life became a subject through which to explore AI-assisted iterative development, rather than a predetermined scientific research objective.

The initial edition developed across November 19–20, 2026. The first recorded numerical CI run began at 04:06:58 UTC on November 19; the automated film publication and repository links were committed by 16:09:07 UTC on November 20, an elapsed interval of 36 hours, 2 minutes and 9 seconds. This is a documented milestone interval within approximately two days of creation, not measured hands-on labor. The authorship essay records the corresponding workflow and commit references.

I invested deliberately little direct effort in evolving the numerical model, relying on short conceptual prompts and AI-assisted implementation. Most of my attention went toward aesthetics, visual presentation and the quality of the resulting artifacts. I intentionally permitted mathematical formulations to exceed my own understanding in order to explore how far a developer could extend an iterative creative process beyond their independent technical and mathematical capabilities.

The numerical chapters should be read in that context: they preserve the models and observations generated during the experiment, not a body of mathematics I claim to have independently derived or fully validated. CI execution, numerical checks and scientific validity are distinct.

## Art as a process of discovery

For me, the aesthetic subject is not a static object but the unfolding of a system. Color, transparency, motion and spatial sectioning make otherwise inaccessible fields perceptible. Patterns are generated by numerical evolution rather than manually arranged. My artistic challenge is to construct conditions under which unexpected forms become visible while retaining a clear distinction between the underlying state and its visual representation.

The transition from two-dimensional chemical patterns to volumetric scenes and four-dimensional sections changes the way the observer encounters the work. A moving section is a view into a higher-dimensional state, not a direct image of the entire space. Identity colors and records introduce an additional observational vocabulary without necessarily changing the dynamics.

## Engineering the conditions for emergence

The numerical foundation consists of dimensionless fields on periodic lattices. Local reaction terms couple substrate and autocatalysts; discrete diffusion and conservative transport redistribute them. Later experiments introduce feedback, replenishable energy, matrix deposition, excitation, inherited variation and ecological interactions. These mechanisms are explicit hypotheses about organization, not assertions that the resulting structures are living organisms.

In my practice, engineering and artistic observation are inseparable: stable integration makes extended observation possible, and instrumentation reveals behaviors that motivate changes to the model. Repeatable initialization, retained source revisions, numerical diagnostics and archived observations allow the visual work to be investigated rather than merely admired.

## Recursive development

Two distinct recursions organize the project. Within an experiment, the state at one integration step becomes the input to the next. Feedback permits the products of activity to influence subsequent activity. Across experiments, the observed behavior and limitations of one model inform the construction of its successor. A generation denotes a model revision and a fresh seeded experiment, not a biological descendant or a continuation from a saved state.

## Numerical foundations

Lattice spacing and integration time step are unity. The discrete Laplacian sums nearest neighbors minus twice the spatial dimension times the center value. Transport uses first-order upwind fluxes; heterogeneous diffusion uses face-averaged coefficients. A prescribed divergence-free velocity is not a momentum-conserving fluid model. Concentrations and energy are dimensionless. Unless stated otherwise, active chemical coverage is the fraction above 0.1 and matrix coverage the fraction above 0.2. Coverage is not mass fraction.

The experiments documented here use seed 42. Single-seed observations establish examples of behavior rather than statistical robustness. Matched controls can isolate selected mechanisms within a fixed experimental configuration, but broader generalization requires ensembles and sensitivity analysis. Overlap-based identities are observational constructs, not biological ancestry.

## Reading the evidence

The reported observations are predominantly single-seed experiments. Active coverage is not mass. Identity events depend on sampling cadence. A successful render establishes execution, not a biological or scientific validation. The report distinguishes analytical examples from measurements and records pending experiments explicitly.
