# Recursive creation

*An artist's statement on the relationship between human intention, computational systems and the unexpected forms they produce.*

## The process as medium

Ontogenesis began as an experiment in possibility: what kind of computational world could develop through successive acts of direction, synthesis and observation, without a complete mathematical specification at the outset? The project took the form of an artificial-life simulation, but its subject is equally the process by which that simulation was constructed. Its mathematical mechanisms, numerical histories, analytical instruments and moving images are components of one work.

I supplied conceptual questions, selected directions and assessed the resulting forms. AI participated extensively in transforming those intentions into equations, software, experiments and representations. The numerical systems were executed by automated computational infrastructure. This distribution of work was deliberate: I wanted to understand what happens when creative intention can guide the exploration of technical complexity that exceeds the creator's capacity to implement or independently derive every component.

That arrangement did not eliminate the need for judgment. It made the boundary between implementation and understanding more visible. I could recognize an image as compelling without claiming that its underlying mathematical explanation was correct. I could ask for a structural mechanism without knowing beforehand whether the simulated field would form a coherent boundary. The world could answer my intention with something unexpected, ambiguous, or empty.

## Three layers of evolution

Three distinct processes provide the conceptual structure of the work.

**Evolution within a numerical world.** Chemical concentrations interact under local update rules. Later models incorporate energy, feedback, heredity and changing environmental structure. These are computational analogues of selected biological processes, not evidence that the system is alive.

**Evolution of the model.** Each numbered generation is a newly initialized experiment. Mechanisms and instruments are revised between generations; the sequence is not a single organism's continuous ancestry. Some experiments add interactions, while others establish controls or refine the capacity to observe.

**Evolution of the inquiry.** Results change the next question. A saturated matrix motivates a different structural measurement; a seemingly persistent region prompts an examination of its thresholds and topology; an attractive moving field invites comparison with native numerical observations.

The significance lies in the relationship among these layers, not in treating them as the same process.

## A recursive creative method

The development cycle can be described in six stages.

1. **Intention:** define a question or desired property of the artificial world.
2. **Synthesis:** formulate and implement a candidate computational mechanism.
3. **Execution:** evolve the numerical state and record observed fields.
4. **Observation:** encounter structures, instabilities and limits of the model.
5. **Evaluation:** examine the artistic character and the available numerical evidence.
6. **Revision:** retain, discard or reformulate mechanisms and instruments.

A revision is informed by the preceding observation, yet it does not guarantee more complex or more biologically meaningful behavior. Some generations produced structural saturation; others produced near disappearance at a selected threshold. Those outcomes are not failures of authorship. They are part of the material that guides the work.

## Authorship, uncertainty and form

Artistic direction and AI-assisted construction are distinct but interdependent. I remain responsible for the questions, aesthetic decisions, interpretation and published presentation. AI contributed substantially to the mathematical and engineering realization. Describing the software as entirely hand-authored would misrepresent the production process; describing Ontogenesis as an autonomous AI artwork would similarly overlook the human choices that shaped it.

The initial creative edition unfolded across November 19–20, 2026. The recorded interval between the first numerical workflow and the first automated film-publication milestone was approximately 36 hours. It is a record of elapsed milestones, not a measurement of human labor or a scientifically validated productivity multiplier.

The distinction between a computed state and its image matters. Projection, sectioning, camera motion, lighting and interpolation can make a four-dimensional field perceptible, but also conceal important properties. Numerical records and controlled comparisons constrain what can be said about a beautiful image. Neither an attractive film nor a passing workflow proves biological organization.

For me, engineering is a creative medium because a system can make possibilities visible that were not specified in advance. The resulting work is not only the collection of images. It is the evolving relation between an intention, a mathematical world that does not simply obey that intention, and the instruments through which the world becomes intelligible.
